*Quick answer: To get your business shown in AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews, you need to (1) make sure AI crawlers can access your site, (2) publish structured, fact-dense content that answers real questions, (3) strengthen your entity and brand signals across the web, (4) implement layered schema markup, and (5) keep content fresh and verifiable. This discipline is called Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), and it builds on, rather than replaces, [traditional SEO](/services/seo).*

Your customers are not just Googling anymore. They are asking ChatGPT which contractor to hire, asking Perplexity which software to buy, and reading AI Overviews before they ever click a blue link. If your business does not show up in those AI-generated answers, you are invisible at the exact moment a buying decision gets made.

Here is the uncomfortable part: strong Google rankings no longer guarantee AI visibility. [Ahrefs found](https://ahrefs.com/blog/) that 28.3% of ChatGPT's most-cited pages have zero organic visibility in Google, and fewer than 10% of sources cited by ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot rank in Google's top 10 for the same query. The signals that get you ranked and the signals that get you cited are diverging fast.

The good news: getting cited by AI is a learnable, repeatable process. This guide walks through exactly how AI engines choose sources and what your business can do to become one of them.

## What Is Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)?

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of improving your brand's chances of being mentioned, cited, or recommended in AI-generated answers across platforms like ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI Overviews. You may also see it called AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) or AI SEO. The labels differ; the goal is the same: when an AI writes an answer in your category, your business is part of it.

The term was formalized by [Princeton researchers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735), whose study showed that targeted optimization can increase a source's visibility in generative AI answers by up to 40%. The highest-impact tactics were adding quotations (+27.8%) and adding statistics (+25.9%). In other words, evidence-dense content wins.

GEO does not replace SEO. Traditional SEO gets your pages discovered and trusted. GEO structures those pages so AI systems can extract, verify, and cite them. Pages ranking in Google's position one still have roughly a 58% chance of being cited in AI Overviews, so the two disciplines compound each other.

## How AI Engines Actually Choose Sources

When someone asks an AI a question like "who is the best HVAC company in Colorado Springs," the model does not run one search. It performs what is called query fan-out: it breaks the question into several sub-queries (best HVAC Colorado Springs, HVAC reviews, emergency furnace repair near me) and retrieves sources for each one. It then synthesizes an answer from short passages pulled across multiple sites, citing the ones that provided clear, verifiable facts.

That process rewards a specific kind of content:

* **Passages that stand alone.** Each section should answer one question completely, without needing the rest of the page for context.
* **Facts with proof.** Specific numbers, credentials, dates, and named sources get extracted. Vague marketing copy gets skipped.
* **Clear entity signals.** The AI needs to know exactly who you are, what you do, and where you operate, consistently, everywhere your business appears online.

## 7 Steps to Get Your Business Cited by AI

### 1. Make Sure AI Crawlers Can Actually Read Your Site

This is the most common failure we see, and it is often invisible to the business owner. Check that your robots.txt is not blocking AI crawlers like GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot. If you use Cloudflare, be aware that its default configuration now blocks AI bots, so your AI crawl traffic may have been shut off without anyone deciding to do it. Also make sure important content is server-side rendered. AI crawlers read the HTML your server returns; content hidden behind JavaScript may as well not exist.

### 2. Lead With Direct Answers

Put a concise, self-contained answer in the first 200 words of every important page, then elaborate below it. AI Overviews cite from the first 30% of a page's content the majority of the time. Start sections with a clear definition or answer sentence before adding nuance. Write the way your customers ask questions out loud, because that is how prompts are phrased.

### 3. Pack Your Content With Verifiable Facts

Replace "we're the trusted local experts" with "we've completed 1,400+ installations across El Paso County since 2012 and hold a 4.9-star rating across 380 Google reviews." Cite reputable external sources. Include statistics, dates, prices where possible, and named credentials. The Princeton research is unambiguous: quotations and statistics are the strongest citation levers available.

### 4. Strengthen Your Entity and Local Signals

AI engines cross-reference your business against the wider web before recommending you. That means your name, address, phone, services, and service area must be consistent across your website, Google Business Profile, industry directories, and review platforms. Reviews matter more than ever: when someone asks an AI for the best provider in a city, the model looks for proof like ratings, review volume, and credentials to justify its recommendation.

### 5. Implement Layered Schema Markup

Structured data is how you speak to machines in their native language. Stack the relevant types on each page: Organization or LocalBusiness site-wide, Article or BlogPosting on content, FAQPage on question sections, and Service or Product where applicable. Schema does not guarantee citations, but it removes ambiguity about who you are and what each page covers, which is exactly what retrieval systems need.

### 6. Build FAQ Sections That Match Real Prompts

Mine your sales calls, support emails, and tools like Google's "People Also Ask" for the literal questions customers ask. Answer each one in 2-4 tight sentences. FAQ content is disproportionately extractable because it is already formatted the way AI answers are: question in, direct answer out.

### 7. Keep Content Fresh and Show It

Stale pages lose citations. Update your cornerstone content on a regular cycle, show visible "last updated" dates, and add new data points as they emerge. Freshness signals tell both crawlers and models that your facts are still trustworthy.

## How to Measure AI Visibility

Traditional analytics will undercount this channel badly. One 2026 study found that roughly 70% of AI-driven traffic arrives without referrer headers, making it invisible in default GA4 reporting. Track these instead:

* **AI citation share:** how often your brand appears when target prompts are asked across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and AI Overviews
* **Share of voice vs. competitors** on the same prompt set
* **Branded search lift:** many AI users read the answer, then Google your name later
* **AI referral traffic** with proper GA4 configuration to capture it

Run a monthly prompt audit: ask each major AI engine 10-20 questions your customers would ask, and log who gets mentioned. If it is not you, that is your roadmap.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between SEO and GEO?

SEO optimizes your site to rank in traditional search results, using signals like keywords, backlinks, and user engagement. GEO optimizes your content to be cited inside AI-generated answers, prioritizing factual density, standalone passages, structured data, and entity clarity. They are complementary: SEO gets you discovered and trusted, GEO gets you quoted.

### How do I know if ChatGPT or Google AI is mentioning my business?

Ask them. Run your key customer questions through ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews each month and record which businesses get named. For ongoing tracking at scale, AI visibility monitoring tools can measure your mention rate and share of voice across platforms. Growth Local includes this tracking in every GEO engagement.

### Does my Google ranking affect whether AI cites me?

Partially. Pages ranking at position one in Google have about a 58% chance of being cited in AI Overviews, so rankings still help. But chat-based engines like ChatGPT pull heavily from sources that do not rank well in Google at all, so ranking alone is not enough and not ranking is not disqualifying.

### How long does it take to show up in AI answers?

Technical fixes like unblocking AI crawlers can produce results within weeks, since chat engines retrieve live web content. Building the entity authority and review signals that earn recommendations in competitive categories typically takes 3-6 months of consistent work, similar to SEO timelines.

### Do small local businesses really need GEO, or is this just for big brands?

Local businesses arguably benefit most. When someone asks an AI "who should I call for X in my city," the model has to name specific local providers, and it chooses based on reviews, consistent business information, and content that proves expertise. A well-optimized local business can outcompete larger brands in its own service area.

### Will blocking AI crawlers protect my content?

It will mostly just remove you from the answers. If GPTBot or PerplexityBot cannot read your site, those engines cannot cite you, and your competitors who allow crawling become the sources instead. For most businesses, visibility is worth far more than exclusion.
